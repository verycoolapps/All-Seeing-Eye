# Architectural Review

## When to use
Use when reviewing architectural imagery at building scale — facade photos, elevation drawings, site plans, massing studies, competition renders, or real buildings requiring design critique.

## Inputs required
- One or more images of the building (photo, render, or drawing)
- Optional: project type (residential, commercial, cultural, infrastructure), climate/locale, program brief
- Optional: scale reference (floor count, site photos, surrounding context)

## Steps
1. **Identify the building typology and read its intent.** What is it trying to be — monument, background fabric, shelter, statement? Judge it against its own ambition level first.
2. **Massing and proportion.** Assess the overall volume: does the massing logic cohere? Are proportions (bay spacing, floor-to-floor, solid-to-void ratios) consistent? Flag awkward junctions, unmotivated cantilevers, and scale errors (elements too small/large to read correctly).
3. **Facade analysis.** Read the facade as a system: rhythm of openings, hierarchy of entrances, material transitions, ornament density. Check whether detail reinforces or fights the massing.
4. **Material and texture.** Judge material choices for appropriateness to climate, program, and budget context; assess texture legibility at viewing distance; flag mismatched or token materials.
5. **Context relationship.** Does the building respond to street, landscape, neighbors, and sun path? Note ignored opportunities (blank walls facing public space, entrances hidden, no shading strategy).
6. **Interior hint from imagery.** If visible: circulation clarity, daylight penetration, spatial hierarchy. If not visible, mark interior quality as not assessable.
7. **Score all dimensions** on the rubric; weight Functional Quality and Contextual Appropriateness higher for architecture.
8. **Prioritized critique.** List issues by severity: structural/proportion errors, contextual failures, material problems, polish opportunities. Reference the decision matrix for recommended changes.

## Output format
```
PROJECT: <type + intent>
MASSING: <assessment>
FACADE SYSTEM: <assessment>
MATERIALS: <assessment>
CONTEXT: <assessment>
SCORE TABLE: <10 dimensions>
ISSUES BY SEVERITY: <critical / major / minor>
VERDICT: <design rating + one-line summary>
```

## Confidence handling
- HIGH: clear elevation views, known program, good image quality.
- MEDIUM: single-angle view only, or render without material realism — note what cannot be verified (structure, interior, construction).
- LOW: aerial-only or heavily stylized imagery — restrict claims to massing and silhouette; mark facade and material detail as not assessable.
