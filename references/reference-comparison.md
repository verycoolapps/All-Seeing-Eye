# Reference Comparison

## Purpose
Enable systematic comparison between two or more visual references — target vs. actual, before vs. after, concept vs. execution — identifying gaps, improvements, and regressions.

## Core knowledge
- **Comparison types:** target vs. actual (did we hit the mark?), before vs. after (did we improve?), concept vs. execution (did we realize the idea?), A vs. B (which is better?), series progression (did consistency hold?).
- **Systematic dimensions:** composition/framing, color palette, lighting, texture/material, detail level, style consistency, technical quality, emotional tone, brand alignment.
- **Gap analysis:** for each dimension, assess: match (same), better (improved), worse (regressed), different (changed intentionally?), missing (absent in one), extra (added unexpectedly).
- **Reference roles:** reference can be inspiration (adapt, don't copy), specification (match exactly), benchmark (meet or exceed), anti-reference (avoid these traits).
- **Visual diffing:** identify specific regions that changed; quantify change if possible ("lighting is 30% warmer," "composition shifted left"); don't just say "looks different."
- **Regression detection:** improvements in one area may cause problems in another; check that fixing lighting didn't break color balance, etc.
- **Intent vs. accident:** distinguish deliberate deviations (artist's choice) from unintentional errors (mistakes); ask or infer from context.
- **Progression tracking:** for series/seasons, check consistency (same style, same quality bar) and evolution (intentional changes vs. drift).

## Evaluation criteria / rules
- **Always compare dimension-by-dimension**, not holistically; holistic comparison misses specific regressions.
- For each dimension, state: status (match/better/worse/different/missing/extra) + brief observation.
- Quantify when possible: "exposure is 1 stop brighter," "saturation is reduced by ~20%," "rule-of-thirds alignment shifted from center to left-third."
- Identify the 3 most significant differences (highest visual impact) and 3 most significant similarities (what stayed consistent).
- For "which is better" comparisons: evaluate each against stated criteria, then synthesize; don't just pick favorites.
- Flag when references serve different purposes: "Image A is a mood reference (inspiration), Image B is a technical spec (must match) — they can't both be 'correct.'"
- For before/after: celebrate improvements, flag regressions, note unchanged issues.
- Always ask: "What was the goal? Match exactly, improve, or take inspiration?" — comparison criteria depend on intent.

## Confidence & limits
- High confidence: measurable differences (exposure, color values, composition geometry), obvious regressions, clear improvements.
- Medium: "feels more cohesive," "better matches reference," subjective quality comparisons.
- Low: intent attribution (was this deliberate?), cultural context of references, long-term brand consistency.
- Cannot compare: images of different subjects/contexts meaningfully, works requiring domain expertise, references you can't see clearly.
- Always state: "Comparison based on visible features; underlying intent or process unknown."
