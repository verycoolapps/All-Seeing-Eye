# Color Intelligence

## Purpose
Analyze and apply color with precision — color theory, harmony systems, psychological effects, technical color management, and the difference between pleasing palettes and meaningful color choices.

## Core knowledge
- **Color theory:** hue (position on wheel), saturation (purity), value/brightness; primary (red, yellow, blue), secondary (orange, green, violet), tertiary; complementary (opposite = high contrast), analogous (adjacent = harmony), triadic (three equidistant), split-complementary, tetradic.
- **Color harmony rules:** 60-30-10 (dominant/secondary/accent), analogous schemes (5 hues same family), complementary with dominance (80% neutral + 20% accent), monochromatic (one hue varying value/saturation).
- **Color psychology:** red (passion/danger/energy), orange (warmth/enthusiasm), yellow (optimism/caution), green (nature/growth/calm), blue (trust/calm/sadness), purple (luxury/creativity), pink (femininity/playfulness), brown (earth/stability), black (power/elegance/mourning), white (purity/sterility).
- **Cultural color meanings:** white = purity (Western) vs. mourning (some Asian); red = luck (China) vs. danger (Western); color symbolism varies by context and culture — always flag when relevant.
- **Color temperature:** warm (red-orange-yellow, advances, energetic) vs. cool (blue-green-purple, recedes, calm); warm colors come forward, cool recede in space.
- **Simultaneous contrast:** colors appear different depending on surroundings; a gray looks warm next to blue, cool next to red; isolated color swatches mislead.
- **Technical color:** RGB (additive, screens) vs. CMYK (subtractive, print); sRGB vs. Adobe RGB vs. ProPhoto; gamma/linear workflow; color profiles for web/print/screen.
- **Accessibility:** colorblindness (8% of males, deuteranopia most common); never rely on color alone for information; WCAG contrast ratios (4.5:1 text, 3:1 large text).

## Evaluation criteria / rules
- Identify palette: dominant hue, accent colors, neutral base; classify harmony type; assess saturation/value balance.
- Check color relationships: do colors clash or complement? Are contrasts intentional or accidental?
- Evaluate color psychology vs. context: does red mean danger or passion here? Does the palette serve the mood/brand?
- For design: specify colors with names + hex/RGB values; avoid "blue" when "navy" or "sky blue" is meant.
- Flag color errors: oversaturated primaries (cheap), muddy browns (over-mixed complements), neon accents on neutrals (harsh), all-cool or all-warm palettes (monotonous).
- Consider accessibility: sufficient contrast, colorblind-safe palettes, not color-only information encoding.
- For photography/rendering: white balance affects all colors; grading shifts hue relationships; check skin tones remain natural.

## Confidence & limits
- High confidence: obvious color harmony violations, technical color errors (wrong profile, oversaturation), clear psychological associations.
- Medium: "pleasant palette," cultural color meanings, brand alignment.
- Cannot assert: exact color values from images (without sampling), colorblindness impact without testing, cultural appropriateness without user context.
- Always distinguish color harmony (technical) from color meaning (cultural/contextual).
