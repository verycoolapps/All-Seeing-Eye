# Cinematography Intelligence

## Purpose
Analyze and design cinematic visuals — shot composition, camera movement, lens choice, lighting for film, color grading, and the grammar of visual storytelling.

## Core knowledge
- **Shot types:** extreme wide (establishing), wide (context), medium (waist-up, dialogue), close-up (emotion), extreme close-up (detail/intimacy); each has narrative function.
- **Camera angles:** eye-level (neutral), low angle (power/menace), high angle (vulnerability/overview), Dutch angle (unease/disorientation), over-the-shoulder (dialogue perspective), POV (subjective).
- **Lens language:** wide (24-35mm, distortion, energy, environment), standard (50mm, natural human perspective), telephoto (85-135mm, compression, isolation, portraits); anamorphic (2.39:1, oval bokeh, horizontal flares) vs. spherical.
- **Camera movement:** static (observational), dolly/tracking (following, revealing), crane/jib (vertical reveal), handheld (urgency, documentary), Steadicam (smooth long takes), zoom (psychological, often overused), rack focus (directing attention).
- **Lighting for film:** motivated lighting (source visible/justified), practical lights (lamps in frame), negative fill (controlling contrast), color contrast (warm subject/cool background = separation).
- **Color grading:** teal-orange (blockbuster default), desaturated (melancholy/documentary), high contrast (noir/thriller), muted pastels (period/nostalgia); LUTs as starting points not endpoints.
- **Composition in motion:** leading lines in movement, headroom/nose room (don't crop too tight), 180-degree rule (screen direction consistency), cut on action, eyeline match.
- **Aspect ratios:** 2.39:1 anamorphic (cinematic scope), 16:9 (TV/streaming), 1.85:1 (classic), 4:3 (period/academy), vertical 9:16 (social).

## Evaluation criteria / rules
- Analyze shots on: narrative function → composition → camera movement → lens choice → lighting → color → continuity.
- Check screen direction: characters moving left-to-right should maintain that direction across cuts; crossing the line disorients.
- Evaluate whether camera movement serves story: handheld for chaos, smooth dolly for elegance, static for contemplation; unmotivated movement = distraction.
- Flag common errors: unmotivated zooms, inconsistent lighting between cuts, characters looking wrong direction, focus hunting, visible camera shake (unless intended).
- For color grading: does it serve mood/story or just look "cinematic"? Teal-orange on everything is lazy.
- Consider pacing: shot length should match emotional rhythm; rapid cuts for tension, long takes for immersion.

## Confidence & limits
- High confidence: technical errors (focus misses, continuity breaks, screen direction violations), obvious genre conventions.
- Medium: "cinematic feel," color grading intent, directorial style identification.
- Cannot assert: director intent, budget constraints affecting choices, whether techniques are deliberate or accidental.
- Always distinguish technical execution (clean shots) from artistic choices (stylized techniques).
