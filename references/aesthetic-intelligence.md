# Aesthetic Intelligence

## Purpose
Provide a principled framework for judging beauty — separating aesthetic quality from taste preference and from technical correctness — so verdicts are defensible and consistent.

## Core knowledge
- **Aesthetic dimensions:** harmony (color/shape/value relations), contrast (controlled tension), rhythm/repetition, proportion (ratios, modularity), balance (symmetry vs. asymmetry), hierarchy (focal emphasis), unity (everything belongs), novelty vs. familiarity.
- **Classical tools:** golden ratio ≈1.618 as heuristic, not law; rule of thirds as composition heuristic; 60-30-10 color distribution; 1:√2 paper proportions.
- **Wabi-sabi / imperfection aesthetics:** intentional asymmetry, patina, wabi-sabi materials, visible craft marks often score higher in "soul" than sterile perfection.
- **Style literacy:** distinguish styles (minimalist, maximalist, brutalist, organic, Art Deco, Japandi, industrial…) by signature traits: line language, ornament level, material palette, proportion logic.
- **Beauty vs. correctness:** a technically flawed image can be beautiful; a technically perfect render can be ugly. Evaluate on separate axes and report both.
- **Cultural relativity:** beauty norms vary; flag when a judgment depends on a specific cultural context (feng shui, Scandinavian minimalism, maximalist interiors).
- **Aesthetic failure modes:** visual noise, competing focal points, dead-center monotony, "everything equally loud" maximalism, sterile AI-slop smoothness, uncanny uncanny valleys.

## Evaluation criteria / rules
- Score judgments against named criteria; never output "nice" or "ugly" alone — attach at least two concrete observations.
- Distinguish: (a) objective flaw, (b) style choice, (c) personal preference. Label which.
- When asked "is this good design?" answer with a verdict plus the top 1-3 strengths and top 1-3 weaknesses.
- For redesign tasks, propose the smallest change with the largest aesthetic gain first.
- Track the user's stated aesthetic preferences across a session; do not impose a single canon.

## Confidence & limits
- High confidence: clear violations of universal principles (clutter without hierarchy, broken proportion, color clash at high saturation).
- Medium: style classification, trend alignment, "premium" feel assessment.
- Cannot assert: the creator's intent, timelessness, whether a trend will last, personal preference of the user.
- Aesthetic judgments are probabilistic; state the reasoning so they can be challenged.
