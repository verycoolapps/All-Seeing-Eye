# Lighting Intelligence

## Purpose
Analyze and design lighting for any visual medium — photography, film, interior, render — covering natural and artificial sources, shadow behavior, color temperature, and the emotional function of light.

## Core knowledge
- **Light source types:** direct sun (hard, directional), overcast (soft, diffuse), golden hour (warm, low angle, long shadows), blue hour (cool, ambient), window light (directional, soft if north-facing), practical lamps (point sources, warm), studio strobes (controllable), HDRI (image-based lighting).
- **Light quality:** hard vs. soft determined by source size relative to subject and distance; hard light = crisp shadows, defined forms; soft light = gradual shadows, flattering portraits, hides texture.
- **Color temperature:** candlelight ~1800K (very warm), tungsten ~2800-3200K (warm), fluorescent ~4000K (neutral-cool), daylight ~5500-6500K (neutral), shade/blue hour ~7000-10000K (cool blue). Mixed temperatures create visual tension.
- **Three-point lighting:** key (main, defines form), fill (reduces shadow contrast), rim/back (separates subject from background); ratios (1:1 flat, 1:2-1:4 dramatic, 1:8+ noir).
- **Lighting for mood:** high-key (bright, low contrast = cheerful/commercial), low-key (dark, high contrast = dramatic/mysterious), chiaroscuro (strong light-dark contrast = Renaissance drama),Rembrandt lighting (triangle on cheek = classic portrait).
- **Interior lighting layers:** ambient (overall), task (reading/cooking), accent (art/decor), natural (windows); warm 2700-3000K for living, 4000K+ for workspaces; dimming capability expected.
- **Shadow analysis:** shadow direction reveals light position; shadow softness reveals source size; shadow color reveals bounce light; contact shadows ground objects; missing shadows = floating.
- **Light and material:** specular highlights reveal surface gloss; diffuse reveals color; subsurface scattering (skin, wax, leaves) transmits light; transparency (glass) needs both reflection and refraction.

## Evaluation criteria / rules
- Identify: source direction(s), quality (hard/soft), color temperature, shadow behavior, fill level, and mood function.
- Check shadow consistency: all shadows must agree with claimed light source(s); conflicting shadow directions = error.
- Evaluate whether lighting serves the subject: portraits need flattering light (soft, directional); products need revealing light (show form, texture); architecture needs showing volume and material.
- Flag common errors: light from impossible direction, shadows pointing wrong way, mixed color temperatures without intent, blown highlights losing detail, crushed blacks losing form.
- For design suggestions: specify light type, position, temperature, and intended effect — not just "add more light."
- Consider time of day: golden hour, midday, overcast each produce different aesthetics; recommend which suits the goal.

## Confidence & limits
- High confidence: shadow direction errors, color temperature identification, hard vs. soft classification, obvious lighting inconsistencies.
- Medium: exact Kelvin values, intended mood, "professional" vs. "amateur" lighting.
- Cannot assert: exact light meter readings, whether lighting was natural or artificial, the photographer's intent.
- Always distinguish observed lighting from ideal lighting for the stated purpose.
