# Composition Intelligence

## Purpose
Analyze and apply composition across all visual media — photography, painting, film, UI, architecture — covering framing, balance, rhythm, and the deliberate arrangement of elements.

## Core knowledge
- **Composition frameworks:** rule of thirds (grid 3x3, place key elements on intersections/lines), golden ratio (1:1.618 spiral/rectangles), rule of odds (odd numbers feel natural), rule of space (leave room in direction of movement/gaze), symmetry vs. asymmetry, leading lines (guide eye), framing (use environment to frame subject), depth layering (foreground/midground/background).
- **Balance types:** symmetrical (formal, stable, static), asymmetrical (dynamic, interesting, requires weight distribution), radial (centered, ceremonial), crystallographic (pattern-based).
- **Visual weight:** size (bigger = heavier), color (saturated/dark = heavier), texture (busy = heavier), isolation (alone = heavier), position (lower = heavier in Western reading).
- **Rhythm and pattern:** repetition (creates unity), alternation (A-B-A-B), progression (increasing/decreasing size), radiating (from center); rhythm creates movement and interest.
- **Negative space:** empty areas that give breathing room, emphasize subject, create tension; too much = sparse, too little = cluttered; Japanese concept of ma (negative space as meaningful).
- **Tension and stability:** diagonal lines = dynamic tension, horizontal = calm/stability, vertical = strength/aspiration, curved = organic/friendly; balance stability with enough tension to stay interesting.
- **Crop and framing:** headroom (space above head in portraits), nose room (space in direction of gaze/movement), lead room (space in front of moving subject), tight vs. loose framing (intimacy vs. context).
- **Architectural composition:** facade rhythm (window spacing), massing hierarchy (primary/secondary volumes), void/solid ratio, entrance emphasis, roofline variation.

## Evaluation criteria / rules
- Evaluate composition on: focal point clarity → balance → rhythm → negative space → tension/stability → cropping → overall harmony.
- Check rule of thirds: are key elements on intersections or lines? Is horizon placed intentionally?
- Assess visual weight distribution: is one side too heavy? Does it feel balanced or tipping?
- Look for leading lines: do they guide eye to focal point or distract?
- Evaluate negative space: is there enough breathing room? Does empty space serve purpose or feel accidental?
- Check cropping: are important elements cut off? Is there enough context? Does crop serve subject?
- For UI: visual hierarchy through size/position/color; F-pattern/Z-pattern reading; whitespace for grouping.
- Flag composition errors: dead-center monotony, unbalanced weight, no clear focal point, cluttered frame, awkward cropping, broken lines.

## Confidence & limits
- High confidence: rule-of-thirds violations, obvious imbalance, no focal point, cluttered composition.
- Medium: "dynamic composition," "static feel," style appropriateness.
- Cannot assert: photographer's intent, cultural composition norms without context, whether rules are deliberately broken for effect.
- Always distinguish rule-following (technical) from rule-breaking (artistic choice).
