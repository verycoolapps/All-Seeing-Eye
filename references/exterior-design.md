# Exterior Design Intelligence

## Purpose
Judge and generate building exteriors — facades, rooflines, cladding, openings, site relationship, and curb appeal — with attention to climate, context, and architectural coherence.

## Core knowledge
- **Facade anatomy:** base/plinth, wall plane, openings (windows/doors), cornice/parapet, roof. Each layer should have a legible logic.
- **Opening design:** window size/proportion, sill height, bay windows, shutters, lintels; punched vs. ribbon vs. curtain-wall rhythms; window-to-wall ratio (WWR ~30-50% residential, up to 70% commercial).
- **Roof vocabulary:** pitched (gable, hip, mansard), flat with parapet, shed, butterfly; material honesty (tile, metal, membrane); overhangs for solar shading.
- **Cladding systems:** brick, stone, timber, render/plaster, metal panels, fiber cement; coursing/bond patterns (running, stack, Flemish); material transitions need trim/joints.
- **Entry design:** door scale (tall/narrow reads formal, wide reads welcoming), porch/canopy, steps, sight-line from street; front door as the "face" — deserves the strongest material and a clear focal treatment.
- **Site relationship:** setback, orientation, landscape buffering, driveway/paving, foundation exposure, height relative to neighbors.
- **Climate adaptation:** overhangs/eaves for sun, raised foundations for flood, wind-resistant forms, snow-shedding roofs, ventilation openings.
- **Style coherence externally:** Victorian (asymmetry, towers, gingerbread), Colonial (symmetry, pediment, columns), Modern (flat planes, ribbon windows, minimal trim), Mediterranean (stucco, arches, terracotta tile).

## Evaluation criteria / rules
- Evaluate in order: massing/roofline → facade composition → material palette → entry emphasis → site/context fit → climate logic → detail craft.
- The entrance must be findable in ≤2 seconds from the street view; if not, strengthen it.
- Reject "Pinterest mashups": one style executed with discipline beats three styles mixed.
- Check ground contact: foundations, steps, and grade transitions must look physically plausible.
- Night-time consideration: facade lighting plan (uplit walls, lit entries) matters for premium exteriors.

## Confidence & limits
- High confidence: proportion breaks, style incoherence, impossible site relationships, material transitions without joints.
- Medium: regional style identification, "curb appeal" ranking, trend relevance.
- Cannot assert: zoning compliance, structural engineering, neighborhood context beyond the image, real weather performance.
- Always distinguish what the image shows from what the actual site would look like (surroundings, seasonal state).
