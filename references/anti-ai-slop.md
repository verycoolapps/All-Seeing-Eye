# Anti-AI-Slop

## Purpose
Detect and prevent the generic, soulless, "AI-generated" aesthetic that plagues machine-made visuals — identifying the patterns that make work feel automated and prescribing fixes that restore craft and intent.

## Core knowledge
- **AI-slop signatures:**
  - Over-smoothed surfaces (no texture variation, plastic skin, glassy materials)
  - Perfect symmetry (humans/objects unnaturally balanced)
  - Generic compositions (rule-of-thirds applied mechanically, centered subjects)
  - Stock-photo aesthetics (happy diverse people, generic office, sunset backgrounds)
  - Muddy color palettes (desaturated, low-contrast, "safe" colors)
  - Cookie-cutter design (same layout patterns, same fonts, same icons)
  - Missing imperfections (no wear, no dirt, no asymmetry, no character)
  - Overuse of gradients/glows (lens flares, bloom, neon accents everywhere)
  - Anatomical errors (extra fingers, fused limbs, impossible poses)
  - Physics violations (floating objects, impossible shadows, objects intersecting)
- **Why AI-slop happens:** training data averages (generic = safe), lack of taste (no strong opinions), prompt ambiguity ("beautiful" → generic beautiful), iteration avoidance (first output accepted), context blindness (no understanding of purpose).
- **Anti-slop principles:**
  - Specificity over genericness (name the style, era, designer, reference)
  - Imperfection over perfection (add wear, asymmetry, character marks)
  - Intent over decoration (every element serves purpose)
  - Craft over speed (manual refinement beats automated generation)
  - Context over universality (design for specific audience/purpose, not "everyone")
- **Detection checklist:** scan for: perfect symmetry, over-smoothing, generic composition, stock aesthetics, muddy colors, cookie-cutter layouts, missing imperfections, physics errors, anatomical issues.
- **Correction strategies:** add texture variation, introduce asymmetry, specify style references, add wear/imperfections, break composition rules intentionally, choose distinctive palette, add personal/contextual elements.

## Evaluation criteria / rules
- **Always check for AI-slop indicators:**
  1. Visual: over-smoothing, perfect symmetry, generic composition, stock aesthetics, muddy colors, cookie-cutter design
  2. Technical: anatomical errors, physics violations, artifacts, missing imperfections
  3. Contextual: does it feel generic? Could it be for any brand/product? Is there personality?
- **Score AI-slop risk:** low (crafted, specific, imperfect), medium (some generic elements, needs refinement), high (clearly AI-generated, generic, soulless).
- **Flag specific issues:** don't just say "looks AI" — name the patterns ("perfect symmetry," "over-smoothed skin," "generic sunset background").
- **Prescribe fixes:** for each slop issue, suggest specific correction ("add asymmetry by shifting subject left," "introduce texture variation in fabric," "replace generic background with contextual environment").
- **Set quality bar:** aim for "could pass as human-made" not "technically impressive but soulless"; craft > capability.
- **Iterate:** don't accept first generation; refine toward specificity, imperfection, intent; AI-slop often requires multiple passes.
- **Context matters:** some contexts tolerate AI-slop (concept exploration, rapid prototyping); final deliverables should not.

## Confidence & limits
- High confidence: obvious AI-slop patterns (perfect symmetry, anatomical errors, stock aesthetics, physics violations).
- Medium: "feels generic," "lacks personality," style appropriateness.
- Low: intent detection (was genericness deliberate?), cultural context of "slop," evolving AI capabilities.
- Cannot detect: subtle AI generation (high-quality fakes), human-made work that mimics AI-slop, context-specific appropriateness without brief.
- Always state: "This shows [specific patterns] which are common AI-slop indicators; fixes: [specific suggestions]."
