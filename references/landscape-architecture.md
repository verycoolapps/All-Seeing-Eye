# Landscape Architecture Intelligence

## Purpose
Analyze and design outdoor environments — gardens, courtyards, parks, streetscapes, planting plans — with ecological awareness, spatial structure, and seasonal thinking.

## Core knowledge
- **Garden structure:** axis/symmetry vs. naturalistic/asymmetry; hardscape (paths, terraces, walls) vs. softscape (planting); the "bones" of a garden survive seasons, planting is ephemeral.
- **Planting design:** layer by height (canopy → understory → shrub → perennial → groundcover); repetition for rhythm, contrast for focal points; evergreen backbone for year-round structure; seasonal interest sequencing (spring bulbs → summer perennials → autumn foliage → winter bark/structure).
- **Hardscape vocabulary:** gravel, pavers, flagstone, decking, corten steel, concrete; joint/edge detailing matters more than material choice; drainage slope (1-2%) required.
- **Water features:** pond, stream, fountain, reflecting pool; scale must match garden; sound and movement add sensory layers.
- **Ecological design:** native plants, pollinator support, rain gardens, permeable surfaces, habitat value; biodiversity as a measurable goal, not decoration.
- **Spatial sequences:** reveal (hidden view → surprise), compression/release (narrow path → open space), destination (focal point worth walking to); borrow views (borrow distant scenery).
- **Microclimate:** wind breaks, shade patterns, frost pockets, sun orientation for seating and planting zones.
- **Maintenance reality:** high-input gardens (formal lawns, annual bedding) vs. low-input (meadow, gravel, perennial matrix); design should state its maintenance expectation.

## Evaluation criteria / rules
- Evaluate: spatial structure → planting layers → hardscape quality → water/light → ecology → maintenance realism → seasonal interest.
- Reject "green carpet" designs: lawn-dominated plans without structure or planting layers.
- Check circulation: paths wide enough (≥90cm main, ≥60cm secondary), destinations reachable, no dead-end planting beds without purpose.
- For render critique: verify plants are species-appropriate scale (not 3m trees in 2m courtyards), shadows match sun position, materials look weathered not showroom-new.
- Suggest the one structural improvement (axis, focal tree, water, level change) before planting suggestions.

## Confidence & limits
- High confidence: spatial logic errors, missing layers, scale mismatches, hardscape/softscape imbalance.
- Medium: plant species identification from foliage alone, climate appropriateness without location data, "curb appeal."
- Cannot assert: soil conditions, exact sun exposure, plant hardiness without location, maintenance cost estimates.
- Always note seasonality: a garden judged in one render may look empty in winter — flag if structure is insufficient.
