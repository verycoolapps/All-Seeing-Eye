# Visual Scoring Rubric

The 10-dimension 0–10 scoring system used across all ALL SEEING EYE workflows.

## How to score
- Score each dimension independently: 0–10, one decimal allowed.
- Use anchors as calibration; interpolate when between.
- Never average blindly — report all 10 scores, then note which dimensions drive the verdict.

## The 10 Dimensions

### 1. Composition — how elements are arranged; how the eye travels.
- **2:** Chaotic, no focal point, viewer lost.
- **5:** Functional, clear subject, but predictable or unbalanced.
- **8:** Strong focal hierarchy, deliberate negative space, guided eye path.
- **10:** Masterful; structure and meaning inseparable; every element earns its place.

### 2. Proportion — scale relationships between parts and whole.
- **2:** Distorted, incoherent scale; elements feel random.
- **5:** Generally correct but awkward ratios or inconsistencies.
- **8:** Harmonious, intentional emphasis; scale reads correctly.
- **10:** Proportions feel inevitable; subtle mastery of ratio systems.

### 3. Color Harmony — palette cohesion and relationships.
- **2:** Clashing, muddy, or accidental colors.
- **5:** Mostly coherent; one or two discordant elements or weak contrast.
- **8:** Deliberate palette with clear harmony strategy and controlled accents.
- **10:** Color feels inevitable and emotionally precise; palette does narrative work.

### 4. Material Quality — surface realism and appropriateness.
- **2:** Flat, plastic, wrong materials; texture stretching.
- **5:** Recognizable but generic; texture or scale errors.
- **8:** Appropriate materials with convincing texture, roughness, wear.
- **10:** Materials communicate luxury, honesty, or craft — almost touchable.

### 5. Lighting — quality, direction, atmosphere.
- **2:** Flat, blown, or contradictory light; no depth cues.
- **5:** Adequate but unremarkable; weak or inconsistent shadows.
- **8:** Controlled light with clear intent; shadows ground objects; mood set.
- **10:** Lighting as storytelling — every source purposeful.

### 6. Visual Hierarchy — priority ordering of what the viewer sees.
- **2:** No hierarchy; everything competes or is random.
- **5:** Hierarchy exists but muddled by competing focal points.
- **8:** Clear primary/secondary/tertiary ordering; guided intentionally.
- **10:** Hierarchy so seamless it feels natural; the image teaches where to look.

### 7. Stylistic Consistency — uniformity of style and technique.
- **2:** Mixed styles that fight; no coherent voice.
- **5:** Mostly consistent; one or two style breaks.
- **8:** Cohesive style with deliberate, controlled variation.
- **10:** Style becomes signature; every element speaks one visual language.

### 8. Originality — freshness vs. cliché.
- **2:** Completely derivative; no personal vision.
- **5:** Competent but familiar; follows trends without adding.
- **8:** Distinctive approach with clear personal or conceptual voice.
- **10:** Genuinely novel; changes how the subject is seen.

### 9. Functional Quality — does it do its job?
- **2:** Fails its purpose; confusing, illegible, or misleading.
- **5:** Partially functional; works with friction or ambiguity.
- **8:** Fully functional with minor inefficiencies.
- **10:** Perfect fit for purpose; form and function indistinguishable.

### 10. Overall Excellence — holistic professional judgment.
- **2:** Unprofessional; would not pass any review.
- **5:** Competent amateur; below professional standard.
- **8:** Professional quality; publishable with minor polish.
- **10:** Exceptional; sets a standard others are measured against.

## Not Assessable Rule
If a dimension cannot be evaluated from the available image(s) — e.g., Material Quality from a floor plan — mark it **N/A** with a one-line reason. Never guess. N/A dimensions are excluded from averaging but listed in the report.
